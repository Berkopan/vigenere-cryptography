KASISKI EXAMINATION + FREQUENCY ANALYSIS

Turn repetition into a key.

Kasiski reveals likely key lengths, not the key itself. Once the period is known, each key position becomes a Caesar cipher that can be attacked statistically.

Important distinction Kasiski narrows the search space. This lab follows it with per-column frequency analysis to recover the actual key.

Why repetition leaks

If the same plaintext fragment lands under the same section of a repeating key, it receives the same sequence of shifts and produces the same ciphertext fragment.

B

Why distances matter

The distance between matching ciphertext fragments is often a multiple of the key length. Factoring several distances makes that hidden period visible.

C

Why the key can follow

Split the text by that period and every column uses one fixed Caesar shift. English letter frequencies can then expose each shift independently.
